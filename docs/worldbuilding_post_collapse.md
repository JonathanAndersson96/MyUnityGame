Worldbuilding Notes High Fantasy — Working Summary (v20) 
Setting & Tone 
• High fantasy, dark & gritty tone 
• World scale ~Europe-sized (~10-12 million sq km) — revised up from an initial Roman Empire estimate after checking mountain-range-to-continent proportions on the hand-drawn map • Climate gradient: cold north, Mediterranean-style middle, arid/hot south 
History — The Empire 
A former empire once existed and is now fallen. It practiced forced conscription of gods — binding them (true names, blood pacts, captured relics, etc.) to compel them to grant power to soldiers and mages, often against their will, possibly including use in atrocities. The empire's collapse left the land scarred and hostile rather than ending the danger. 
Note: an early "hard powers vs. soft powers" magic framing has since been scrapped and replaced by the channeling system described later in this document. 
Geography — Overview 
• Land is dangerous largely because of the empire's history with the gods • People live in isolated strongholds — dozens across the world, major and minor • Old empire infrastructure (roads, ruins, walls) still connects places but is also dangerous/haunted •Interior of Plate 1 is mixed, not uniformly dangerous or safe 
• Map drawn using the rice method: Plate 1 is the large central landmass; Plate 2 (northeast) and Plate 3 (southeast) are separate landmasses 
Tectonics — Three Plates 
Plate 1 (modeled on the African Plate): the old, stable continental core. Plate 2 (modeled on the Philippine Sea Plate/Japan): young and active throughout, converging from the northeast. Plate 3 (modeled on the Anatolian Plate): older with a calm interior, converging from the southeast. 
Plate 1 ↔ Plate 2 (NE): narrow, winding, island-choked strait (Aegean-style), with a young mountain range inland on Plate 2's side. 
Plate 1 ↔ Plate 3 (SE): earlier-stage, more open convergence (Taurus-style range), gentler/lower, with few or no strait islands. 
Plates 2 and 3 don't interact directly, but their boundary lines with Plate 1 converge and run close together toward the south, off-page. 
Plate 1's Quadrants 
• NW — highlands (ancient eroded shield, rounded/tapering into hills), facing open ocean • N/NE — old mountain belt running roughly north/south, with an old river plain between it and the Plate 2 strait; drains via its own independent river system to the NE coast
• Center-west — the failed rift's origin point 
• Diagonal center to SE — the failed rift, now developed into a proper canyon (upgraded from an earlier shallow-depression concept), running from west-center down to the SE coast, with the Great River running along its floor 
• S coast — an old seabed patch, plus the hotspot track reaching the coast • SW/W — open ocean 
• Other: a second old seabed patch near the NE strait 
Plate 2 & Plate 3 Features 
• Plate 2: young volcanic range inland from the strait, scattered active volcanic zones, a fertile volcanic plain, and rugged highland coastal stretches 
• Plate 3: Taurus-style range inland from the strait (gentler than Plate 2's), a calm central plateau, and a worn secondary mountain range showing its own older history 
Mountain Range Geometry (general principle) 
Subduction-driven mountain ranges form on the overriding plate, set back inland from the trench, and run parallel to the plate boundary. Plate 1 gains no new mountains from either boundary — its own mountains (highlands, old belt) come from separate, older history. 
Rivers & Lakes (finalized against hand-drawn river sketch) • Tributaries gather from the NW Highlands and north/upper-interior, converging into the failed rift • The resulting Great River follows the rift trunk and exits on Plate 1's southeast coast (not into the SE strait itself, and not west as originally guessed) 
• The N/NE quadrant's old mountain belt has its own separate, independent river system draining to the NE coast near the Plate 2 strait, unconnected to the Great River 
• The southern old-seabed area and the hotspot track each have their own local drainage • Lakes: a chain of rift lakes along the failed rift, a crater lake near the hotspot track, and marshy lowland near the old-seabed patches 
Plate Sizes (clarified) 
• Plate 2 is medium-sized (Japan/Philippine Sea Plate scale) 
• Plate 3 is actually the largest of the three landmasses overall — only its northern edge (nearest Plate 1) is mapped/relevant; its own deep south is off-page and not detailed 
• Plate 1 is smaller than originally assumed relative to its neighbors, though this doesn't change anything already built for it 
Climate (corrected) 
An early pass over-applied rain-shadow logic and made too much of Plate 1's interior arid, despite its Europe-like latitude. Corrected version: 
Plate 1 — prevailing westerly winds. NW coast/highlands: heavy orographic rain, temperate rainforest. Most of the interior (including around the failed rift and the Great River): temperate forest, farmland, and
grassland — not desert. Climate shifts gradually toward Mediterranean through the middle latitudes, becoming genuinely arid/desert only in the deep south near the southern coast and hotspot track. 
Plate 2 — cool-to-temperate overall. Wet windward coast facing the Plate 1 strait; drier volcanic interior beyond the range, with fertile volcanic-soil pockets. 
Plate 3 (visible northern portion) — Mediterranean coast near the strait, grading into a semi-arid central plateau; understood as the cooler/wetter end of a much larger plate whose actual deep south isn't mapped. 
The hand-drawn map is now considered essentially finished at the terrain level: mountains, hills, and the canyon are all drawn in, dense mountain clusters on Plate 2/3, hills scattered through the central interior. 
Biome Plan 
• NW Highlands — temperate rainforest, old-growth, damp (Pacific Northwest/coastal Norway feel) • N/NE old mountain belt — mixed temperate forest on windward slopes, drier grassland/scrubland/farmland on the coastal plain facing the Plate 2 strait 
• Central interior/hills (between highlands and canyon) — the biggest, most livable zone: temperate forest grading into farmland and open grassland, likely the empire's old population center 
• The canyon — its own microclimate: lush and damp along the river at the bottom, drier and scrubbier on the exposed rim and upper slopes; good territory for hidden settlements in the walls • Middle latitudes — gradual shift from temperate forest to Mediterranean scrub, drought-adapted vegetation, golden grasses 
• Deep south (near southern coast/hotspot track) — true arid desert/semi-desert, with oasis-like fertility around the hotspot's volcanic soil and local water 
• Plate 2 — dense wet forest on the windward coast, drier scrub/grassland on the leeward volcanic interior, fertile volcanic-soil patches 
• Plate 3 (visible portion) — Mediterranean coastal scrub/woodland near the strait, grading into semi-arid steppe/grassland across the central plateau 
Three Protagonist Concepts 
1. The History-Channel — human, from the Elder-family stronghold. Inherited channel of a Death/Reality-adjacent Knight called the God of History, leans ritual magic. Can read a person's past, see the past of a location, and learn about past rituals/lost knowledge — power and clarity scale with proximity to and effort spent on the source, degrading the further back or more obscured. One of the only plausible ways the true history of the Collapse could ever be uncovered in-story, since Death cannot tell the living. 
2. The Necromancer — an Ursine, willing channel of a Death-adjacent Knight, chosen specifically by the Death Architect itself. Powers: speaking to and animating the dead, plus vast unearned combat experience — not offensive skill, but an almost supernatural defensive ability to parry and avoid attacks, having vicariously experienced countless battlefield deaths and effectively "seen every killing blow before." He doesn't overpower opponents, he simply doesn't get hit. Favors a short, well-balanced thrusting spear, suited to his reactive, read-and-redirect style. Backstory: a genuinely ancient Ursine, predating the god-created races. Deliberately killed by Death itself as part of a ritual to bind him as an
eternal resurrection failsafe, meant to be revived precisely when needed. Has just now awoken — centuries after the Collapse — specifically in response to Chaos's successful binding of Light. Remembers almost nothing of his own past, and doesn't yet know he is Death's failsafe. His one instinct: an inexplicable pull toward the high priest. His true, not-yet-self-known purpose is to free Light. 
3. The Chieftain's Child — a Dwarf, from one of the free/breakaway Dwarven clans who chose to live independently from the mainstream hive-minded, king-ruled Dwarven society, settled in the northwestern hills and mountains. These free clans retain only a weaker version of the Dwarven hive-mind, maintained through heavy, deliberate rituals rather than effortlessly. They organize via "tings" (Norse-style open assemblies), an intentional cultural echo of Ursine jarl-clan culture. The protagonist's murdered parent was chieftain of one of the most important free clans, amid a long-standing blood feud with the mainstream Dwarven king. The king is the primary suspect (may be the true culprit or a red herring, undecided). A deliberately grounded, non-channel contrast to the other two protagonists. 
Cosmology — The 13 Architects 
The infinite pantheon exists beneath 13 foundational gods called the Architects. They are "presences" that manifest wherever their fundamental force is present in the world. Family structure: Fire, Earth, Water, Air are siblings; Life, Death, Matter, Spirit are a second sibling quadruplet; Order, Chaos, Light, Shadow are a third; all three quadruplets are cousins to each other. Reality stands apart as the founder/first-among-equals who invited the other twelve into existence. 
Reality 
Controls space and time; eternal and everywhere, but not part of anything. Brought the universe into being. Mostly passive, exists to observe (god of curiosity). Cannot break the other gods' rules because it is those rules. Is empowered by the other twelve collectively, and so is passively/structurally weakened by Order's death (and Earth's, if that death is retained) — not directly wounded by the Collapse itself, but weakening as a slow side-effect. Does not know it is Chaos's true target. 
Order (dead) & Earth (wounded) 
Order was god of structure, conservation, power, kings, planning, law, and crafting (helped create immensely powerful artifacts, now irreplaceable). Grew in power in places of great cohesion — the empire itself was the ideal trap, which is what made Order vulnerable enough to be killed in the Collapse. Order's death is confirmed, settled lore. Earth was the force behind plate tectonics, earthquakes, and mountains, able to conjure or prevent them at will; also god of meditation, labour, and belonging. Resolved: Earth is not dead, but was severely wounded by an ongoing plot of Chaos — this wound is what caused a major present-day earthquake, killing the History-channel protagonist's father and older brother. The world's geology is erratic and dangerous as an ongoing symptom of Chaos's continued scheming, not just a one-time Collapse-era event. 
Chaos 
Orchestrated everything: helped one kingdom build the empire specifically to bind gods, let it run for generations, then triggered the Collapse to kill Order as a step toward its true ongoing goal of destroying Reality. Also responsible for a separate, ongoing plot that has severely wounded Earth (mechanism/details left open). Twist: Chaos is actually a human soul in a god's body — after helping create humans, it deliberately gave up its own divine spirit to break the rules binding the other Architects,
and so cannot be bound or channeled by anyone. Retains all its original memories and personality, but no "duty" holds it back anymore. Its body is immortal and indestructible. Some other Architects reportedly already know about this. Present-day plan (mechanism undefined): gathering the strongest channelers toward an eventual ritual to kill Reality. 
Light & Shadow 
Light is god of speed, co-god of the sun (with Fire and Matter), kindness, and bravery — but also fanaticism and extremes. Powers: teleportation, conjuring light, inspiring genuine good, but also driving people to fanatical extremes. Was permanently bound against its will into a human collaborator of Chaos, who has survived since the Collapse and now poses as high priest of a surviving empire-remnant stronghold, publicly leading a changed version of the old religion while secretly worshipping Chaos. The priest has access to a fraction of Light's powers, read by followers as holiness rather than suspicion. 
Shadow is god of winter, night, selfishness, stealth, and balance (correcting wrongs, tipping scales back toward fairness). Went mad and abandoned its post after failing, with Light, to stop Chaos — no longer maintains proper winter darkness or night (seasons drifting: longer/brighter summers, shorter/less dark winters), and no longer performs its balance-correcting function either. 
Fire & Water 
Fire (Light's cousin): calm and extremely slow to anger, but when finally provoked is like nothing ever seen. Powers: fans/amplifies any flame (including the sun), causes magma eruptions, calls down lightning, conjures fire from nothing at grand scale, plus rousing courage and making people feel safe. Co-created the sun with Light and Matter; people unknowingly venerate Fire just by keeping hearths lit. 
Water: erratic, never stops or conforms, with three simultaneous co-present "faces" matching its physical phases — Ice (cold, rigid, stubborn), liquid Water (fleeting, playful), Vapor (erratic, quick to anger, disconnected from the world). Powers: rivers, currents, rain, snow; also drives curiosity and exploration, and is the god of giving. 
Air, Life & Death 
Air is deliberately kept mysterious: almost never present anywhere, rarely interacts with people or gods. Powers: wind, storms, reflecting light, levitation, creating vacuums; also god of respite, birds, and hospitality. Since sibling Earth died, Air has been forced into more interaction than it's comfortable with, and is notably awkward at it. 
Life is a genuine exception to the matter/spirit non-interaction rule: always present in its own single, separate, immortal physical body. Merciless and bloodthirsty, the apex predator of all living things, enjoying the hunt in service of survival-of-the-fittest. Powers/domains: creates life, present at every birth, helped create the world's races, god of sickness, plants, survival, competition. 
Death is calm and peaceful, like a nurse at a dying person's bedside; genuinely emotionally invested, feeling/living everything every dying creature feels, everywhere, simultaneously — the "All-Knowing God." Truly immortal (like Life). Can interact with the dead but cannot take lives; omniscient but cannot interact with the living at all — likely why Death has never told anyone the truth about Chaos/the Collapse, despite knowing it firsthand. 
Matter & Spirit
Matter is the embodiment of everything physical; god of the body, channeling, and ritual. Has no active willful power and cannot be channeled itself ("cannot channel the god of channeling") — it does not act, it simply is the physical world. Backstory: Matter used to be a normal person/Architect with will like the others, but gave that up entirely and permanently during the world's creation — arguably the greatest sacrifice among the 13. 
Spirit is the true equal/opposite of Matter: god of magic, soul, religion, faith, and incantations; present in everything with a soul; retains full will and overwhelmingly powerful magic; worshipped as the "Soul-Giver." Matter and Spirit together contributed the two fundamental kinds of magic to creation (Spirit = gods' essence, Matter = physical bodies/the tangible world). 
The story is set several centuries after the Collapse. Living memory is gone, records are fragmentary/contradictory, and most of this true history (Chaos's true nature and plan, Death's knowledge, Light's captivity, Reality's slow weakening) is lost or unknown in-world. 
Magic System — Channeling Mechanics 
Two kinds of magic exist: magic of the gods (a god acting entirely on its own, within its own domain, unchanneled) and channeled magic (a god acts through a person, granting a fraction of its power and some of its personality). 
Five channel types: 
• Lifelong — a god chooses one person for life; the person becomes immortal •Inherited — power passes down among relatives; on the channel's death the god jumps to the most worthy kin, or dies if the bloodline ends 
• God-forced — the god compels a person against its will 
• Willing — a person willingly enters the bond 
• Person-forced — a person forces themselves onto an unwilling god; rare, requires real ritual/knowledge (mostly lost since the empire) or help from another god 
Immortality is specifically tied to lifelong channels, or otherwise only if the god is powerful enough or grants it some other way. Being a channel means hearing the god in your mind and taking on some personality-bleed, scaling with the god's power and the channel type. Gods choose channels through power, personal merit, or happenstance. 
On the channel's death: willing/lifelong channels free the god; inherited channels jump to worthy kin or the god dies if the bloodline ends; forced channels die along with the person, because forcing a channel binds the god's Spirit directly to that person's Matter — an unnatural override of the normal matter/spirit non-interaction rule. Destroy the matter (the body) and the bound spirit (the god) has nowhere left to 
exist, so it dies too. 
Why the Architects stay distant from world events (and mostly out of the main plot): two layered reasons. (1) Duty/nature — acting outside one's own domain conflicts with an Architect's own sense of role. (2) Fear — the empire's attempt to bind Order failed, and that failed binding is specifically what made Order vulnerable enough to be killed in the Collapse. Binding/overreach is an existential threat to an Architect, not just a taboo, and the survivors know it. 
Power Hierarchy: Architects & Knights
Simplified to two tiers (a "Commanders" middle tier was tried and then scrapped as an unnecessary layer). Architects are essentially never bound/channeled — catastrophic if attempted — except it has now happened twice: the empire's first attempt to bind Order failed, and that failure is what actually caused the Collapse (revising the earlier vaguer framing); centuries later, Chaos successfully bound Light into the high priest. Knights are the only tier below Architects: infinite in number, deliberately uncatalogued even in-world, spanning a wide power range themselves rather than a fixed sub-tier. A Fire-adjacent Knight might produce lava, inspire truth-telling, or call thunderstorms — a reduced slice of the parent Architect's power, plus something uniquely their own. 
Two Methods of Performing Magic: Rituals & Incantations 
Rituals (Matter's domain) produce long-lasting, geographically-bound, or physically-summoned effects — wards, lasting barriers, summoned objects/creatures — and require physical materials to perform; slower and more deliberate. Incantations (Spirit's domain) produce immediate, conjured effects; the god literally speaks through the channel, making incantations far more demanding on the body and personality than rituals, tying directly into the existing cost mechanics. All channels can use both methods, though individuals tend to lean toward one. 
Vertical Categorization 
Cuts across the three horizontal sibling quadruplets, renamed Elements (Fire, Earth, Water, Air), Power (Life, Death, Matter, Spirit), and Morals (Order, Chaos, Light, Shadow). Each vertical group takes one member from each: Physical = Earth, Order, Matter; Ethereal = Air, Shadow, Spirit; Destruction = Fire, Chaos, Death; Vitality = Water, Life, Light. Reality stands outside all groups, horizontal and vertical alike. 
The World's Races 
Six races established so far: 
• Elementals — created jointly and simultaneously by the four Elements Architects (Fire, Earth, Air, Water) to shape and protect the world. Semi-humanoid, human-level intelligence; do not eat or need sustenance, and do not reproduce — the population created at the world's founding is fixed forever. Have vast conjuration powers. After the Collapse, many went mad from the destruction of natural environments and are now very dangerous. Whether this is one unified race or four sub-types tied to each parent element is not yet decided. 
• Humans — created by Chaos; destructive and greedy by tendency, not absolute — individuals can and do resist or transcend this. Stocky, ~2 meters tall on average; excellent traders and warriors; average channelers. Many were part of the empire; some human sects still secretly worship Chaos today. Whether anyone in-world knows humans were created by Chaos specifically is not yet decided. 
• Dwarves — created by Order; hive-minded, ruled by a supreme king as ultimate authority. Almost entirely incapable of channeling, though genuine rare exceptions occur and would be remarkable, notable figures. Master craftsmen, echoing Order's own domain; very hard, almost rock-like skin, but not good warriors — mostly laborers. Were also a large part of the empire. 
• Ursines — not created by any Architect; developed naturally through evolution, with some evolutionary pressure from Life. Bear-like, ~3 meters tall, opposable thumbs on hand-like paws, fur concentrated on chest/armpits/groin/head, bear-like molars, large claws, very muscular. Live
mostly in the north; capable channelers, culturally shamanistic and master ritualists. Organized into clans led by jarls, not a hive-mind or unified state. 
• Avians — modeled on corvids (ravens/crows/magpies); towering ~2.5m humanoids, pitch-black skin, raven-like taloned hands/feet with opposable thumbs, wings emerging from the back below the shoulder blades, full bird head. Every Avian is a channeler, each bonding with a different god/Knight — this alone makes them seem god-like to other races. Before the empire, existed as many tribes loosely bound in a confederacy called "a Murder." Nearly all flocked to the empire voluntarily for the chance to channel more powerful gods, and were almost wiped out in the Collapse as a result. Extremely rare today; a surviving remnant lives in a hidden, mage-tower-like stronghold, treated as myth by most other races. 
• Dracosaurs (working title) — dinosaur-dragon hybrid, humanoid/bipedal, warm-blooded; nocturnal specifically to manage their own body heat in their hot southeastern homeland (biologically plausible: warm-blooded desert animals are often nocturnal to shed metabolic heat, unlike cold-blooded animals that bask to warm up). Were driven from their lands by the empire and have been trying to reclaim it ever since; now live scattered along the coast/islands/water rather than holding a unified homeland. Worship the Architects but call them "the Great Dragonlords"; their channelers are called "Dragons," who often focus their magic through their breath (fire/frost/etc. depending on the bonded god/Knight) — a distinct channeling method not yet established for other races. Hate both humans and dwarves over the empire's conquest of their lands. 
Flora & Fauna 
General rule: near-universal gigantism across animals and plants, caused by Life pushing all living things toward evolutionary extremes for millennia. Gigantism is least apparent in the old imperial heartland (the canyon/Great River area) — that region is instead haunted/cursed and empty of normal wildlife (something else can be encountered there: undead, spirits, mad Elementals, or Chaos-touched remnants, exact nature left open) — and most apparent in wilder regions further from historical empire control, since the empire's dominance suppressed it. 
• A giant crocodile-raptor hybrid apex predator (name scrapped, TBD later) — can hunt anything, ambushes prey like a crocodile but hunts in coordinated social packs like raptors, planning ambushes in advance. Ranges across temperate/warm regions, but not the canyon, the hills, or the north. 
• Giant, fist-sized bees, closely tied to Ursine culture for honey; dangerously poisonous stings. Ursines use smoke and incense (tying into their ritualistic culture) to safely calm them for harvesting — a valuable, dangerous, culturally-specific craft other races would need Ursine help to access. 
Open Plot Point: The Dracosaur Sacrifice 
The Dracosaurs have recently performed a horrifying ritual sacrifice to gain an edge in their war to reclaim their homeland: they sacrificed an entire generation's worth of unhatched eggs to a volcano, using the sacrifice to forcefully person-force-bind gods/Knights in the nearby area against their will. Whether this creates new Dracosaur channelers en masse, weakens/destabilizes the local gods to hurt an enemy, or both, is left open. This echoes the empire's own original sin in miniature, and given their already-small population post-Collapse, is an existential gamble.
Setting Era & Tech Level 
The story is set at a Late Bronze Age tech level. The empire itself had already reached an iron age level before it fell, but that knowledge was lost in the Collapse — current society is re-discovering iron, not inventing it for the first time. Iron-smelting requires much higher, more sustained heat than bronze; a rare Dwarven Fire-channeler has begun smithing iron by using channeled Fire heat to get forges hot enough — very rare, and arguably one of the most practically important individuals in Dwarven society right now, for purely technological (not magical) reasons. 
Strongholds sit on old empire ruins in a mix of ways: some are literal reoccupied ruins people still live within, others are newer settlements built from scavenged imperial stone and memory of what stood there. 
Naming Conventions 
Humans lean Latin (e.g. Remulos, Julius, Flavian, Julia, Ottila); Dwarves lean Eastern European/Ukrainian/Russian; Ursines lean Norse/Nordic; Avians and Dracosaurs use more invented, imaginary-sounding names with no real-world linguistic anchor. 
The History-Channel Protagonist's Stronghold 
Sits atop old empire ruins in the southern fertile farmland valley (part of Plate 1's biggest, most livable central biome zone); relatively safe by continental standards but still dangerous outside the walls. Fairly well-traveled by continental standards, though trade is not super common. 
Governed by five Elders forming an oligarchy, most of whom are channelers. They rotate one Elder into a Rome-consul-style dictator role, which can be vetoed if all other Elders unanimously disagree. Which gods/Knights Elder families tend to channel is realpolitik and happenstance, not custom — it's about who's best at politicking, not divine alignment. The protagonist's own family is one of the five ruling Elder families, with royal ancestry; his channel (the God of History) is inherited within this line. 
Inciting incident: a major earthquake (caused by Chaos's wounding of Earth) kills the protagonist's father and older brother, leaving him next in line but politically weak — drawing realpolitik threats from the other Elders and rival to-be-noble families, presumably prompting him to eventually flee the stronghold. 
Flagged for later, not yet developed: a large political entity spanning the entire continent, and "the Travellers' Guild." 
Bronze Age Weapons, Warfare & Mounts 
Standard Bronze Age toolkit: spears (the real workhorse of infantry), short bronze swords, battle axes, maces/clubs, daggers, composite bows, slings, javelins; bronze/leather shields, bronze helmets, leather-or-bronze-scale armor (no full plate). Given the world's mountainous/canyon/forest terrain, chariots don't suit it well, so spear/bow infantry and light skirmish riders matter more than chariots. 
Stirrups do not exist yet (historically accurate for this era, over a thousand years too early) — no couched-lance cavalry charges, no stable mounted melee platform; mounted combat is limited to horseback archery/javelins rather than heavy shock cavalry. Treated as an intentional, historically-grounded constraint. 
Mounts: “Hochs”
A horse/auroch hybrid breed (working name "hochs") fills the horse-equivalent role — likely large and tough, given real aurochs' aggressive power plus the world's general gigantism. Race-by-race: Humans are the primary riders. Dwarves are too small to practically ride hochs, and use donkeys instead. Ursines don't ride hochs, but use them for labor/hauling. Avians don't ride — they fly instead. Dracosaurs don't ride either, despite having no wings and no flight of their own — hochs instinctively fear their predator shape and scent and won't tolerate them as riders, cutting Dracosaurs off from the continent's major mount-based trade/transport/messaging network entirely, compounding their other disadvantages. 
Stronghold Defenses & Population 
Strongholds cannot sustain large populations via distant, undefended farmland if it's genuinely dangerous outside the walls — farmland must be kept close to and defensible by the walls (fortified farming villages, patrols, escorted work parties), which caps realistic population size and reinforces genuine isolation between strongholds. 
Walls vs. wards: mundane walls stop creatures/raiders only — they do not stop unbound or mad gods. Protection against divine-level threats comes from separate ritual wards (using the existing ritual magic system, Matter's domain), laid and maintained by whichever channelers a stronghold has access to. A stronghold's true safety depends on ward upkeep and quality, not just wall height. 
The History-channel protagonist's stronghold has a population of roughly 10,000 — genuine major-palace-city territory (comparable to real Mycenae/Hattusa), likely near the top end of what's realistic for this world given its safety and political weight. Other, more exposed or contested strongholds would plausibly be much smaller. 
The Travellers' Guild 
Confirmed to be the same thing as "the large political entity spanning the continent" flagged earlier — not two separate organizations. The only large organization capable of traveling safely between strongholds, and functionally the closest thing to a unifying continent-wide power that exists, despite not being a formal government. "Traveler" is a real, recognized profession (comparable to Witchers/Allomancers in tone), open to all six races, a genuine meritocracy where standing depends purely on what you provide, not race or birth. Being a channeler is an advantage for a Traveler, not a requirement. 
Hidden truth (not known even within the Guild): it is secretly ruled by an immortal god-king — a lifelong channeler bonded to a Knight called the God of Dreams, which sits in the Shadow domain. His goal is simple: pure personal decadence, living in luxury while others do the work. The bond is explicitly symbiotic, not exploitative on the god's side — the God of Dreams gets access to enormous numbers of people through dreams, while the god-king gets luxury and control. He runs the Guild through thralls — intermediaries who are completely mind-controlled via his dream-based power; even high-ranking Guild members likely only interact with thralls, never him directly. He operates from his own hidden stronghold on an island in the ocean between continents, separate from the Guild's public operations. 
Continents & Key Locations 
All continent names are Proto-Norse-derived, since the Ursines were the oldest race and first to name what they encountered. Aujurlandar ("land of the island") is the main landmass (Plate 1), named by the
Ursines, kept in its fuller archaic form consistent with the setting's Bronze Age timeline. Yfirsai ("oversea") is the eastern continent (Plate 2/3), also Ursine-named, from a distance. A third continent exists that the Ursines never reached — found and named by the empire instead (name not yet chosen). A seventh-race concept for this continent (Scythian-inspired, nomadic steppe culture) was explored but the physical form tried (a crocodile-centaur hybrid) was scrapped as insufficiently original; the seventh race remains an open slot. 
The high priest's stronghold (where Light is bound) sits at the outlet of the Great River, where it meets the sea after flowing the length of the canyon — likely one of the most strategically and symbolically important points on the continent.
